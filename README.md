# ousado
Who is this map for?
I wanted to make a map for a conservationist. Originally I was interested in bird migration in the Caribbean, but I was unsuccessful in finding that data. Along the way I found this dataset, which intrigued me: GPS tracking of Ousado, a jaguar who was burned in the 2020 Pantanal fires and released back into the wild with a GPS collar. In environmental sciences, it is rare to see what happens to an animal after it is released into the wild. This map has the ability to show us where the jaguar has moved, and the timeline in which he moved.
The interaction of this map reveals how the jaguar’s home range grows quickly in the first two weeks, then levels off at about 45–55 km², and then grows again from December into January. A static map can show all of his points, but it would be very tangled and would not be able to show the order in which he moved. The slider is able to reflect this change over time. My map uses Roth’s (2017) sequence operator: the slider steps through Ousado’s GPS locations in order, and as it moves, Turf calculates his home range so far. This is information that a single static map cannot show.
The satellite imagery shows the rivers and wetlands where the jaguar would be around. The gold is a contrast point against the satellite background. There are 9 days with no data collection (26 Nov to 5 Dec), which shows up as a jump in the jaguar’s movement. He may have moved during that time without being recorded, and this explains the data’s limits. [added] The home range shape may also include areas he never visited, and this is only one animal over three months. If I had more time, I would add a play button, and possibly add another species in the same area if I were able to download that data.
